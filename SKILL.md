# Short-Form Video Editing (Reels and Shorts)

Edit vertical short-form videos that hold attention to the last second and are ready to publish without fixes. The edit serves one goal: a viewer who stops, watches fully and remembers the brand.

## Build the structure first

- Cut for the hook before anything else. The first two seconds must show the payoff, the problem or a bold on-screen line. Never open with a logo, a slow intro or silence.

- Structure every video the same way: hook, value or story in the middle, one clear call to action at the end. One video carries one message only.

- Keep total length honest to the content. Most reels land between 20 and 45 seconds. Cut anything that does not move the message forward.

## Cut with pace

- Remove dead air, long pauses and repeated takes. A cut every two to four seconds keeps energy without feeling rushed.

- Use pattern interrupts to reset attention: zoom punches, angle changes, b-roll over the talking parts, and on-screen text that lands with the spoken words.

- Sync cuts and text pops to the beat when music drives the video. Off-beat edits feel unfinished even when viewers cannot name why.

- Cover mistakes with b-roll or a zoom, never with a jump that shows. If a section cannot be saved cleanly, flag it for a reshoot instead of shipping a broken edit.

## Add captions and text

- Caption every spoken word. Most viewers watch on mute first, so the video must make full sense with the sound off.

- Keep on-screen text inside the safe zones: away from the top title area, the bottom caption and profile area, and the right-side action buttons. Text that the platform UI covers is text that does not exist.

- Use one font family and a consistent style for captions and headlines across a brand's videos. Big, high-contrast headline text that stays readable on a phone screen at arm's length.

- Never let text overlap faces, products or other text. If two elements fight for the same space, move one or cut one.

## Mix the audio

- Set voice first, then bring music under it. Music supports the voice and never competes with it.

- Normalise loudness so the video sounds right on phone speakers: clear voice, no clipping, no sudden volume jumps between cuts.

- Use licensed or platform-safe music only. A video removed for audio rights is worse than a quieter track.

## Finish with the brand end card

- End every video with the brand's own end card: logo, website and contact, held long enough to read, about two to three seconds.

- Check the end card shows only the posting brand. Another brand's logo, website or phone in the end card is a reject, not a fix later.

- Use the brand's approved logo file only, on its approved background. No stretched logos, no square backdrops, no recoloured versions.

## Export and check

- Export vertical 1080 by 1920, H.264, with the frame rate of the source footage. Check file size against the platform's upload behaviour before handoff and compress large files without visible quality loss.

- Watch the exported file fully before delivering it, start to finish, exactly as a viewer will see it. Checking the timeline is not checking the video.

- Verify in that final watch: hook lands in two seconds, captions match the speech, no text is covered or overlapping, audio is clean, end card is the right brand and the last frame is not black.

- Name the delivered file with the content's unique code and title so it can be tracked through approval and posting. Keep the project file and the exported file together until the post is live.

## Alternatives for better results

- When raw footage is weak, fix the structure around it: a stronger hook line, tighter middle and a clear end card rescue more videos than effects do.

- Study three high-performing videos in the same niche before editing a new format. Match their pacing and caption style, not their content.
